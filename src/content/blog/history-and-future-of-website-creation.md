## The Dawn of the Web (1991 - 2000)

If you wanted to **create a www website** in the 1990s, you had to learn HTML. There were no visual builders, no CSS, and certainly no AI. Every **create page** action required opening a simple text editor, writing raw angle brackets, and hoping the browser didn't crash. 

During this era, **making websites** was restricted entirely to computer scientists and hobbyist coders. The sites were purely informational—black text on a gray background with blue hyperlinks. To **setup website** hosting meant running your own physical server in a closet.

## The CMS Revolution (2001 - 2010)

The early 2000s democratized the internet with the invention of the Content Management System (CMS), most notably WordPress (2003). 

Suddenly, you didn't need to write HTML for every single page. You could write a blog post in a text box, hit "Publish," and the CMS handled the code. This was a massive leap forward for **create websites** at scale. However, customizing the design still required hiring a PHP developer. **Website sites** all began to look exactly the same, confined to rigid grid layouts.

## The Drag-and-Drop Era (2011 - 2020)

As the demand to **making websites** visually appealing grew, the industry shifted to visual page builders. Platforms like Squarespace, Wix, and eventually Webflow allowed designers to drag elements onto a canvas. 

This was the first time a non-developer could truly design a custom site. But it came with a heavy cost: performance. Visual builders generated notoriously terrible, bloated code. Furthermore, as the mobile web exploded, forcing a drag-and-drop canvas to be responsive became a nightmare. 

## The AI Web Generation Era (2021 - Present)

We are now living in the fourth major era of web development: Artificial Intelligence. 

The question is no longer "How do I code this?" but rather, "How do I prompt this?" We have entered the age of **ai created websites**, where natural language is the ultimate programming language. 

When you use an **ai that creates websites**, you bypass the limitations of the previous three eras. You get the speed of a CMS, the visual control of a drag-and-drop builder, and the clean code of a hand-written site.
